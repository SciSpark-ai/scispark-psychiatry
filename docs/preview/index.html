How it works

The feed is a fixed pipeline. A model is used in four places only (extra queries, grading, the card summary, the topic overview), and code checks what it returns.

  1. Plan and retrievePubMed queries are built from your topics through a psychiatry term pack. The fixed plan always runs; the model may only add queries. Records are merged and kept locally.
  2. FilterPsychiatric records are kept, by MeSH headings and the pack's words, and every drop is logged with its reason. PubMed's publication type decides the study design when there is one.
  3. Grade and selectThe model grades how well a paper matches each topic. A quote it gives must appear in the title or abstract. Recency, design and journal band rank the list; places are shared between topics.
  4. SummariseA headline and one summary sentence per paper. A paper whose summary fails its checks is shown with its own title and the authors' conclusion.
  5. Evidence pagesThe Wiki's three sections (Treatments, Safety, Other research) are built from the papers the feed has listed, with treatments matched to the term pack.

The checks that keep the model honest

  • Numbers must be printed in the abstract. A headline or summary with a number the paper's text does not contain is refused, asked for once more, then dropped.
  • A summary rests on a sentence code looks up. The model names the number of an abstract sentence; it does not quote.
  • A sample size must be tied to people. “180 patients” counts; “12 weeks in patients” and “42 of 180” do not.
  • Association wording is not a benefit outside trials and reviews: “was linked to” or “predicted” shows nothing to have helped.
  • Treatment names are matched to a term pack (names, synonyms, brands), not taken from the model's wording.

Status and limits

  • An early build used by its author. For literature awareness only: not medical advice, and not a tool for deciding the care of a patient.
  • Direction is one label per paper (benefit, no benefit, mixed), an AI reading of the abstract. It can be wrong. There are no effect sizes and no forest plots.
  • Only what your feed listed is counted. A treatment with no bubble may have a large literature the feed never saw.
  • Most papers are read from the abstract alone. In one check of 25 recent papers, about one in five had open full text.
  • Do not enter patient information. No guard against protected health information is built; what you ask is sent to your model provider.
  • Your model key is stored in plain text in the local vault, and local profiles are not access control. English only; the term pack covers psychiatry only.

Run it locally

You need Node.js 20.9 or later, npm, and a model provider key of your own.

git clone https://github.com/SciSpark-ai/scispark-wiki-psychiatry.git
cd scispark-wiki-psychiatry
npm ci
npm run dev

Open http://127.0.0.1:3000. Then: Settings → Connect your AI, answer the onboarding chat, add the conditions and treatments you follow under My topics, and press Refresh on What's new. Every refresh makes model calls with your key. The README has the rest.
